\begin{abstract}
Several documents can support the same claim while ultimately relying on one observation. Counting
such documents as independent corroboration can overstate evidential strength even when every
individual citation is relevant. We introduce EchoBench-Synthetic v1.1, a gold-by-construction
benchmark for source-family inference. It contains 2,640 cases from 240 world-disjoint fictional
micro-worlds crossed with 11 provenance conditions and three balanced verdicts. We compare
document-, domain-, lexical-, TF--IDF-, and sentence-embedding-based grouping with EchoGraph, an
interpretable logistic pair classifier using links, attribution, text overlap, numbers, domains, and
chronology. Thresholds are fixed on 30 validation worlds and evaluated once on 60 held-out worlds.
On the 600 determinate test cases, EchoGraph obtains family-pair F1 of \EchoFOne\ and root-count MAE
of \EchoMAE, compared with F1 \UrlFOne\ and MAE \UrlMAE\ for treating URLs as independent. A
prespecified Bayesian sensitivity analysis shows mean probability error of \SimEchoProbErr\ for
EchoGraph-derived counts versus \DocProbErr\ for document counts. Ablations reveal that links help,
while removing numeric overlap unexpectedly improves aggregate pair F1 to \NoNumFOne. The study is
CPU-reproducible and makes no claim of human validation, open-web provenance recovery, or improved
end-to-end language-model answers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A research system may cite four pages and describe a conclusion as repeatedly confirmed. If three
pages summarize the first, the answer contains four documents but one informational origin. This is
\emph{false corroboration}: apparent support is increased by counting dependent reports as though
they were independent observations. Citation entailment alone does not expose the problem because
each page can genuinely contain the claim.

Source dependence has long mattered in data integration \cite{dong2009dependence}, and citation
networks can amplify claims through repeated references \cite{greenberg2009citation}. Modern
citation and research benchmarks measure answer correctness, citation entailment, evidential force,
and long-horizon synthesis \cite{gao2023alce,du2025deepresearch,qian2026force}. These are necessary
properties, but they do not directly measure how many distinct observations a set of supporting
documents represents. Recent work on source sensitivity in multi-source medical RAG further shows
that source choice can change answers \cite{li2026transplantqa}. EchoTrace targets the narrower
controlled problem of recovering dependence structure among already identified evidence documents.

Evaluating this problem on the open web is difficult: true transmission paths are often missing,
shared datasets may be invisible, and similar wording is not proof of copying. We therefore begin
with a synthetic benchmark in which provenance is known by construction. The benchmark varies
lineage, attribution, wording, domain, shared datasets, and independent replication while holding
the focal task format fixed. It also includes ambiguous cases where the correct action is to
abstain, although our determinate partition metrics exclude them because no unique family count is
defined.

This paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item EchoBench-Synthetic v1.1, with 2,640 public/gold-separated cases, balanced verdicts, opaque
  identifiers, and world-disjoint train/validation/test splits;
  \item a CPU-feasible comparison of source-family baselines and EchoGraph, including six feature
  ablations and whole-world bootstrap intervals; and
  \item an explicit Bayesian sensitivity analysis quantifying the confidence error induced by
  document counting under stated priors and likelihood ratios.
\end{itemize}
The contribution is a controlled component study. It does not establish real-world provenance,
publisher reliability, plagiarism, factual truth, or improved generated answers.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Citation and research-agent evaluation.}
ALCE evaluates correctness, fluency, citation recall, and citation precision in attributed answers
\cite{gao2023alce}. Deep-research benchmarks extend evaluation to long reports and cross-source
reasoning \cite{du2025deepresearch,xie2026deepweb}, while evidence-force evaluation distinguishes
mere relevance from whether evidence warrants a claim's strength \cite{qian2026force}. Audits of
deep-research citations also show that a displayed citation need not provide verified attribution
\cite{onweller2026cited}. EchoTrace adds a different axis: relationships \emph{between} supporting
sources.

\paragraph{Dependence and diversity.}
Source-dependence models for conflicting-data integration infer copying relationships to avoid
double-counting repeated values \cite{dong2009dependence}. MMR reduces redundancy by balancing
relevance and novelty \cite{goldstein1998mmr}, but semantic diversity is not evidential
independence: independent studies may use similar language, while derivatives may paraphrase.
LineageRAG organizes evidence and reasoning provenance \cite{zheng2026lineagerag}; EchoTrace instead
predicts equivalence classes of documents that share an underlying informational origin.

\paragraph{Repeated evidence in agent systems.}
Bara \cite{bara2026sybil} studies evidence ancestry and report multiplicity using a formal
aggregation model and controlled language-model extractions. That work also separates wording
similarity from ancestry. EchoTrace's narrower contribution is a document-family inference
benchmark with visible links, attribution, and shared-dataset cues. Its simulation is not the
first demonstration that dependent reports can distort confidence, and its detector requires
observable signals rather than recovering arbitrary hidden ancestry.

\section{Task Definition}
For a focal claim $c$, let $\D_c=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}$ be its relevant evidence documents. In a
determinate case, a latent mapping $z(d)$ assigns each document to one source family. The target
partition is
\begin{equation}
  \F_c=\{F_k\}_{k=1}^{K},\quad F_k=\{d\in\D_c:z(d)=k\},
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of informational origins, not the number of URLs or publishers. A method
returns $\widehat\F_c$ and $\widehat K$.

We report root-count mean absolute error
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MAE}_K=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\widehat K_i-K_i|
\end{equation}
and exclusive-family pair F1. The latter converts a partition into the set of unordered document
pairs assigned to the same family, computes precision and recall against gold pairs, and assigns
one when both sets are empty. Because this convention rewards correct all-singleton predictions,
we also report each condition separately.

Source-family dependence is not truth or reliability. Two independent studies can share bias; a
derivative report can accurately summarize excellent evidence. EchoTrace only asks whether
documents rely on the same underlying report, study, dataset, sample, or observation.

\section{EchoBench-Synthetic v1.1}
\subsection{Construction}
EchoBench contains 240 fictional micro-worlds. Each world instantiates one of 12 topic templates,
one focal quantitative claim, and all 11 provenance conditions in Table~\ref{tab:conditions}.
Every case contains 12 shuffled documents: four claim-relevant documents and eight neutral
distractors. The relevant documents express evidence consistent with one of three verdicts:
supported, contradicted, or insufficient. Each verdict occurs 880 times and is balanced within
every split.

Documents use reserved \code{.example} domains and opaque random identifiers. Public JSONL contains
only queries and documents. Evaluator-only JSONL contains relevance, verdict, pair relationships,
lineage edges, families, conditions, worlds, and splits. The generator seed is 20260911. Complete
worlds are assigned to train, validation, or test (150/30/60 worlds), preventing a world's matched
conditions from crossing splits.

Gold labels are deterministic consequences of the generator, not human judgments. Structural
validation checks counts, unique opaque IDs, verdict balance, public/gold separation, relevant
pairs, and split isolation. A nearest-centroid probe using mean year, unique-domain count, URL path
shape, and identifier length predicts the 11 hidden conditions with accuracy 0.182 (chance and
majority baseline 0.091), below the prespecified failure threshold 0.55. This limited probe does not
show that all template shortcuts are absent.

\subsection{Evaluation scope}
The component experiment supplies the four gold-relevant documents to every method. This isolates
family inference from retrieval and verdict classification. Ambiguous cases are excluded, leaving
1,500 training, 300 validation, and \TestCases\ test cases. Thresholds are selected on validation
by minimizing root-count MAE, breaking ties by pair F1 and then proximity to 0.5. No test metric was
computed before the protocol, data, code, model revision, and thresholds were SHA-256 frozen.

\section{Methods}
\paragraph{Baselines.}
URL counting assigns every document to a singleton; all-one assigns one family. Domain grouping
uses exact domains. Bag-of-words and within-case TF--IDF methods join pairs above a cosine threshold
and return connected components. The semantic baseline uses normalized embeddings from
\code{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, pinned to commit \code{1110a243fdf4} (the full 40-character revision is
stored in the freeze manifest). Validation thresholds are 0.50 for bag-of-words and MiniLM and 0.95
for TF--IDF.

\paragraph{EchoGraph.}
For each time-ordered document pair, EchoGraph computes direct-link presence, attribution cues,
token Jaccard, five-gram Jaccard, bag-of-words cosine, numeric overlap, same-domain status, and
clipped time separation, plus an intercept. A logistic classifier is trained on the six pairs from
each determinate training case with the target $\ind[z(d_i)=z(d_j)]$. Training uses full-batch
gradient descent, $\ell_2$ regularization, and no language-model calls. At inference, pairs above the
validation-selected threshold 0.50 are joined; connected components define families. Ablations
retrain after zeroing links, attribution, lexical features, numbers, chronology, or domain.

\section{Confidence-Inflation Simulation}
The second experiment translates family-count errors into an interpretable consequence. Let prior
probability be $p_0$, prior odds $O_0=p_0/(1-p_0)$, and $L$ the likelihood ratio contributed by one
independent family. For verdict $v$ and count $k$,
\begin{equation}
 p(H\mid v,k)=\frac{O_0 L^{s(v)k}}{1+O_0 L^{s(v)k}},
\end{equation}
where $s(v)=1$ for supported, $-1$ for contradicted, and 0 for insufficient. The oracle uses gold
$K$; each method substitutes $\widehat K$. We cross priors
$\{0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,0.90\}$ with likelihood ratios $\{1.5,2,3,5\}$. These values are a
sensitivity grid, not estimated real-world parameters.

Outcomes are absolute probability error relative to the oracle and absolute log-odds error,
$|s(v)(\widehat K-K)\log L|$. Insufficient cases have $L^0=1$ and therefore zero error. For every
metric, we average within a micro-world and resample the \TestWorlds\ complete test worlds 10,000
times to obtain percentile 95\% intervals. Paired method contrasts use the same world-level unit.

Removing links reduces F1 from \EchoFOne\ to \NoLinksFOne\ and increases MAE to \NoLinksMAE,
showing that explicit linkage is useful on this construction. Removing attribution has a small

Unexpectedly, removing numeric overlap improves F1 to \NoNumFOne\ and slightly lowers MAE to
\NoNumMAE. Shared numerical findings across dependent and independent documents are one possible
explanation, but the aggregate ablation does not isolate that mechanism. This post-test finding
motivates a future comparison on fresh data; it does not justify selecting the best ablation on
this test set and reporting it as a newly confirmed model.

\section{Limitations and Responsible Use}
The principal limitation is synthetic construct validity. Text is templated, structures are finite,
and labels follow generator rules. Repeated patterns can make held-out worlds easier than unfamiliar
real prose, while hidden lineage may sometimes be underdetermined from visible documents. The
metadata probe covers only four coarse features. No human quality audit was completed, and the
prepared real-source ledger contains no observations; consequently, this paper makes no claim of
human validation or open-web generalization.

A post-freeze construction audit of training data found 577 of 2,250 lineage edges with a
source publication timestamp later than its derivative. This inconsistency can affect both
chronology features and the direction used to detect links. The frozen results are retained as
measured, but chronological realism and mechanism claims require a corrected release and fresh
evaluation. A chronology-valid v1.2 candidate was subsequently generated, but it inherited the
same prose templates and is not evaluated here. A diagnostic AI review of that candidate also
found that exact wording alone did not justify a same-origin label and that its purported
unattributed multihop lineage was not observable from public text. A separate v1.3 candidate
makes republication links visible and changes unattributed repeated reports to an abstention
target. Neither candidate has completed blinded human review, a frozen protocol, or held-out
evaluation; neither contributes any result to this paper.

The experiment assumes gold relevance and therefore does not evaluate retrieval, distractor
rejection, answer generation, citation entailment, or calibration of an actual research assistant.
MiniLM's all-one-like result reflects this benchmark and thresholding procedure, not a broad verdict
about sentence embeddings. Connected components also force non-overlapping families, whereas a
real article may combine several studies or datasets. Claim- and span-level overlapping provenance
will require a richer representation.

Finally, dependence must not be used as a proxy for truth, quality, or misconduct. Similar language
alone cannot establish copying, and a shared family can still be reliable. EchoTrace should not be
used to accuse authors or rank publishers. Any real-source extension should preserve uncertainty,
respect access and copyright restrictions, and obtain independent review where feasible.

\section{Reproducibility}
The repository includes the deterministic generator, public/gold data separation, validators,
validation-selected thresholds, exact MiniLM revision, CPU evaluation scripts, raw JSON reports,
and a freeze manifest containing SHA-256 hashes of 13 study files. The manifest records both
one-shot test runs as complete. All numerical values in this paper are generated directly from the
frozen JSON artifacts. The experiments require Python, NumPy, CPU PyTorch, and
sentence-transformers; no CUDA GPU or paid API is required.

\section{Conclusion}
EchoBench-Synthetic v1.1 provides a controlled test of whether multiple supporting documents
represent distinct informational origins. On its held-out synthetic cases, EchoGraph substantially
outperforms URL, domain, lexical, TF--IDF, and MiniLM grouping baselines, and its family counts reduce
error in a prespecified Bayesian confidence simulation. The results also expose important failures:
shared-dataset structure remains difficult, numeric overlap is counterproductive, and synthetic
success cannot establish real provenance reasoning. The next evidential step is not to pool
candidate-release results with these findings, but to complete blinded human review, freeze a
corrected protocol and test set, and evaluate an end-to-end research-assistant setting. The
appropriate conclusion is therefore narrow: source-family structure is measurable and consequential
under controlled assumptions, but real-world and end-to-end validation remain open work.
