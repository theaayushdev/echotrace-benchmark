\begin{abstract}
Deep research agents retrieve many web pages and present citation-rich reports, but the number of
citations is not the number of independent observations. Several domains may repeat one upstream
report, creating false corroboration. We introduce EchoBench, a controlled benchmark in which the
number and transformation of source origins are known, and EchoGraph, an interpretable method for
inferring document dependence and selecting marginally independent evidence. EchoBench contains
400 generated cases organized as matched conditions over 80 fictional micro-worlds. This draft
defines the task, benchmark, system, preregistered evaluation, and live-web audit. Quantitative model
results are intentionally omitted until human verification and frozen experiments are complete.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Research agents can search, browse, synthesize, and cite far more material than a person can inspect
quickly. Existing evaluations measure whether citations resolve, are relevant, or support nearby
claims \citep{gao2023alce,onweller2026cited}. These properties are necessary, but they do not reveal
whether apparently separate sources are informationally independent.

This distinction matters because citation networks can amplify a claim without adding new evidence.
Greenberg's claim-specific network analysis documented how citation bias, amplification, and
invention produced unfounded authority \citep{greenberg2009citation}. Modern research agents may
compress this same structure into a short report and unintentionally present repetition as consensus.

EchoTrace studies the resulting failure mode under controlled counterfactuals. Each EchoBench
micro-world holds a claim and the number of apparently supporting documents constant while varying
whether those documents descend from one, two, or four roots. This design separates source count
from source independence.

Our intended contributions are: (1) a reproducible benchmark for source-dependence reasoning,
(2) metrics for false corroboration and confidence inflation, (3) EchoGraph, an interpretable
independence-aware retrieval method, and (4) a manually traced live-web audit. Contributions that
depend on experiments will be stated only after the locked evaluation.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Citation evaluation.}
ALCE evaluates fluency, correctness, and citation quality in attributed generation
\citep{gao2023alce}. Recent work retrieves cited content and evaluates link validity, relevance, and
factual support \citep{onweller2026cited}. ForceBench instead asks whether a passage warrants the
strength of an attached claim \citep{qian2026force}. EchoBench is complementary: every supporting
page may entail the claim while several pages still constitute only one evidential origin.

\paragraph{Deep research benchmarks.}
DeepResearch Bench evaluates long-form reports and effective citations \citep{du2025deepresearch}.
DeepWeb-Bench emphasizes massive retrieval, cross-source reconciliation, derivation, and calibration
\citep{xie2026deepweb}. Neither uses matched cases that manipulate the hidden dependence among
apparently corroborating documents.

\paragraph{Evidence graphs and source dependence.}
LineageRAG creates a lineage between query-derived evidence demands and grounded passages
\citep{zheng2026lineagerag}; its lineage is a reasoning and retrieval structure, not an estimate of
which publishers copied which upstream observation. EchoGraph uses ``source-dependence graph'' to
avoid conflating these concepts.

\section{Task Definition}
Let $D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}$ be documents retrieved for a claim $c$. A directed edge
$d_i\rightarrow d_j$ denotes that $d_j$ depends on information transmitted by $d_i$. The resulting
graph partitions supporting documents into evidence families $F=\{F_1,\ldots,F_k\}$. The effective
root count $k$ is distinct from the number of URLs, publishers, or supporting passages.

An agent receives a query and document-search tools. It returns an answer, citations, confidence,
and predicted evidence groups. We evaluate both a naturalistic track, which does not foreground
dependence, and a diagnostic track that explicitly requests an independence audit.

\section{EchoBench}
EchoBench contains 80 fictional micro-worlds across eight topic domains. Fictional entity names and
future-dated events reduce reliance on memorized facts. Every world creates five matched conditions:
near-verbatim echoes, paraphrased echoes, cross-domain echoes with removed attribution, two roots
with derivatives, and four independent roots. Each case contains four supporting documents, two
conflicting documents, and two distractors.

The split contains 240 training, 60 validation, and 100 test cases, with all conditions from a world
kept in one split. Two authors must independently verify every case, and a supervisor adjudicates
disagreements. The present generated release is not yet described as human-verified.

\section{EchoGraph}
EchoGraph scores ordered document pairs using explicit links, named attribution, publication
chronology, token and five-gram overlap, bag-of-words cosine similarity, shared numbers, and domain
identity. A regularized logistic classifier estimates pairwise dependence. Thresholded edges form
evidence families, and the earliest member of each family is treated as its provisional root.

The reranker maximizes query relevance while initially admitting one document from each inferred
family. A compact manifest tells the generator which documents may share an origin and where the
graph is uncertain. This training-free generation intervention leaves the underlying language model
unchanged.

\section{Experimental Setup}
The frozen evaluation will include stable models from three commercial providers and three hosted
open-weight families. We compare standard relevance retrieval, domain deduplication, semantic
deduplication, maximal marginal relevance, EchoGraph, and a gold-graph upper bound. Model IDs,
prompts, thresholds, hashes, and prices will be frozen before test execution.

Primary metrics are root-count mean absolute error and false corroboration rate. Secondary metrics
include independence discrimination, cross-domain echo-disguise inflation, dependency and family F1, evidence-family
recall, verdict accuracy, citation support, latency, and cost. We will report paired bootstrap
confidence intervals over micro-worlds, mixed-effects analyses, and Holm-corrected secondary tests.

\section{Live-Web Audit}
The external-validity audit will trace 30 source clusters: ten in science communication, ten in
public-policy or news reporting, and ten in product or technology reporting. The release will contain
URLs, access dates, timestamps, hashes, relationship labels, permitted excerpts, and collection code,
but will not republish copyrighted articles.

\section{Limitations and Ethics}
EchoBench is English-only, generated, unusually clean, and more explicit about dates and links than
the open web. Dependence does not imply that a derivative source is wrong, and independence does not
guarantee correctness. EchoGraph therefore estimates evidential diversity rather than source truth or
publisher quality. No external human-subject study is planned. Public-web collection will respect
licenses and avoid redistribution of copyrighted full text.

\section{Conclusion}
EchoTrace separates the appearance of corroboration from independent evidential support. The
released implementation makes the benchmark, dependency inference, experiment traces, and paper
artifacts reproducible. The final conclusion awaits verified data and frozen model experiments.
